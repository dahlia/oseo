U14 Phase A measurements
========================

Every reviewed run below is at 97278fc6 on the Linux host in host.log, on the
deterministic reviewed shard 1/100 (214 paths, 730 native executions) unless
a row says shard 1/25 (856 paths, 2,937 native executions). A reviewed run
without --update serializes the shard it produced and throws when it differs
from the checked-in partitions, so every run listed here reproduced the
checked-in manifest shard exactly: pass=183 expected-negative=14
unsupported=17 at shard 1/100, pass=737 expected-negative=57 unsupported=62
at shard 1/25.

"load" is the host's one-minute load average taken immediately before the run.

  There are exactly two starts per native execution: one zig cc compile and
  link, and the native executable itself. That accounts for the 730
  fixture-linux-x86_64-gnu starts and 730 of the 736 zig starts; the
  remaining 6 are unclassified overhead. The recorder groups starts by the
  executable's basename and keeps no arguments, so it cannot say what they
  were, and more than one caller reaches the compiler under that name:
  besides a harness object build, prepareNativeRuntime runs two zig env
  identity probes while the executor initializes. Blocked time is a derived
  44.6 and 45.5 percent of the runner's reported duration at shard 1/100 and
  51.4 percent at shard 1/25; the share grows because the resident set does.

  Within one run the blocked time tracks the resident set. Taking each group
  at its midpoint, the derived slope is 12.9 ms per GiB across the 450 and
  650 MiB groups of shard 1/100 and 9.3 ms per GiB across the 450 and 850 MiB
  groups of shard 1/25. Both are estimates from grouped medians rather than a
  fit to the individual observations. They are lower than the synthetic
  parent's 22.0 ms per GiB, which is consistent with a V8 heap not being one
  contiguous mapping of touched pages, although this measurement does not
  establish that explanation.


4. Where the resident set comes from
------------------------------------

The recorder samples heapUsed once a second without observing collection
boundaries, so the smallest sample in a window is a proxy for the live set
rather than an observation of it. That proxy grows from a measured 97 to 243
MiB over the 856 paths of shard 1/25, which is an estimated 0.17 MiB retained
per path, and the largest heapTotal sample is 918 MiB against a 1,083 MiB
resident set. heapTotal is the whole V8 heap and does not isolate old space.
The shape is consistent with a resident set dominated by heap reserve for
per-case allocation churn, over a retained component that grows with the path
count, and it does not by itself exclude a leak. If the retained component
does grow with paths, a CI shard of 1,782 paths carries more of it than this
sample and its starts are at least as expensive.

Capping the heap was measured on a contended host (load 8.4 to 11.1) and is
inconclusive; it is recorded only to show the direction and the cost.

  configuration            wall s   maximum rss kB   blocked ms   per start ms
  4 CPUs, default           50.18        1,010,984     12,168.0          8.300
  --max-old-space-size=256  34.17          548,248     12,419.3          8.472
  --max-old-space-size=384  30.71          595,616     10,111.3          6.897
  4 CPUs, 256               61.54          516,820     10,810.2          7.374

The cap halves the resident set and buys back some of the start cost, but
the two 4-CPU rows disagree on the sign of the wall-time change and a cap
sized against a 214-path sample risks an out-of-memory failure on a
1,782-path CI shard. More collector time would explain paying for the
smaller resident set, but these rows record no collector duration and do not
test that. This unit does not propose it.


5. Starting processes from a small helper, or from a worker thread
------------------------------------------------------------------

helper-bench.mjs and worker-spawn.mjs, 300 sequential starts. "blocked" is
time inside the start call, on the main thread for the direct and helper
rows and on the worker thread for the worker row, which is a different
thread and not comparable with the others. The comparable columns for the
worker row are wall time and the whole process's system time.

Linux:

  mode     ballast MiB   rss MiB   blocked ms   wall ms   parent system ms
  direct            50     112.9        1.875     631.9              233.5
  direct           650     716.1       18.846    5732.8             2542.6
  helper            50     103.6        0.009     308.0                2.1
  helper           650     706.5        0.009     308.2                6.8
  worker           650     733.8       31.447    9641.5             4442.5

macOS (Apple M4):

  mode     ballast MiB   rss MiB   blocked ms   wall ms
  direct            50     107.2        0.233     396.3
  direct           650     612.5        0.159     278.7
  helper            50      99.3        0.005     262.3
  helper           650     701.6        0.011     492.4

A helper removes the cost rather than moving it: the parent's system time for
300 starts falls from 2,542.6 to 6.8 ms. The page tables copied are then the
helper's rather than the parent's, which is the intended explanation but not
something these two rows separate from other differences. A worker thread
shares the parent's address space, so the same page tables are still copied.
It is measurably worse than starting directly on the two comparable columns,
at 9,641.5 ms of wall time against 5,732.8 and 4,442.5 ms of process system
time against 2,542.6; its 31.447 ms figure is worker-thread time and is not
comparable with the direct row's 18.846 ms of main-thread time. A second heap
to copy would explain the increase, and this measurement does not test that.
On macOS the helper also lowers blocked time, from a measured 0.233 to
0.005 ms and from 0.159 to 0.011 ms, but from figures already under a
quarter of a millisecond, and wall time moves in opposite directions between
the two macOS configurations. Those rows demonstrate no macOS saving either
way.


6. Reviewed A/B with the measurement prototype
----------------------------------------------

ab.sh. The arms differ only by the environment variable that selects the
helper, so no rebuild happens between them and they alternate run by run.
Every run reproduced the checked-in manifest shard. Every individual run,
with its own wall time, runner duration, and host load, is preserved in
ab-runs.tsv.txt in the order the driver ran them; the means below are
arithmetic means over the complete pairs of each group, and each per-pair
percentage compares the two runs of one repetition.

Cache conditions were not controlled. The Oseo compiler cache, which
XDG_CACHE_HOME locates, and the lane's ZIG_GLOBAL_CACHE_DIR both persisted
across every run; the driver neither reset nor warmed them and recorded no
cache state, and it ran base before helper in every repetition rather than
reversing the order within a pair. Warming that continued across the
sequence would land on the helper arm, so these changes are the difference
between the arms as they were run and do not isolate the helper's effect
from cache state. The evidence against such a trend is weak: base wall time
does not fall monotonically across the repetitions of three of the four
groups below.

  configuration            pairs   base wall s   helper wall s   change
  3 CPUs, shard 1/100          3         53.82           50.11    -6.9%
  4 CPUs, shard 1/100          3         45.24           40.87    -9.7%
  16 CPUs pool 8, 1/100        3         32.93           29.57   -10.2%
  4 CPUs, shard 1/25           3        180.60          157.00   -13.1%

    3 CPUs, 1/100:      -7.0%  -6.2%  -7.5%   (load 4.6 to 6.0)

  All twelve pairs are reductions. The saving is larger on the four times

  mean resident set during shard 1/25 is a measured 657.0 MiB against 589.8
  and 604.3 MiB during shard 1/100, and a CI shard is 1/12 of the corpus.

  running, at loads between 9.8 and 12.5, gave -7.4 and +6.7 percent. They
  are recorded in ab-runs.tsv.txt because they were taken, not because they
  measure anything: at that load the arms differ by less than the host does.
  The three repetitions in the table above replace them and were taken at
  loads between 4.3 and 10.3.
